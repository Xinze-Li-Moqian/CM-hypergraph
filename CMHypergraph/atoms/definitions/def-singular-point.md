# Definition — singular point

The seam of a surgery: where the newly glued material meets what survived, the space-time is neither a product nor a half-open product — these most singular points form the scar left by the cut.

## Statement

> [!definition] Singular point
> Let ${\mathcal M}$ be a [[def-spacetime|space-time]]. A point of ${\mathcal M}$ is a *singular point* if it is neither a [[def-smooth-point|smooth point]] nor an [[def-exposed-point|exposed point]].

## Notes

> [!note]- Notes (click to expand)
> ![[singular-corner.svg|695]]
>
> - In a surgery space-time the singular points form the frontier separating the closure of the exposed region from the rest of its time-slice — the seam along which new material is sewn to old. In the flows constructed for the main theorems this seam is a disjoint union of $2$-spheres: the spheres along which the necks were cut.
> - Strictly speaking exposed points are also "singular" in the colloquial sense — they are not smooth — but the word is reserved for the most singular points, where even a one-sided product structure fails.
> - The local model at a singular point (a corner-like chart, the third type of the chart axioms) is part of the surgery-chapter debt.

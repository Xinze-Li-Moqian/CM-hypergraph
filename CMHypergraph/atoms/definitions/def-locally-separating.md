# Definition — locally separating surface

Two-sided versus one-sided: does a small neighborhood of the surface fall
apart into two sides when the surface is removed, or does going around it
bring you back on the other side?

## Statement

> [!definition] Locally separating surface
> An [[def-embedding|embedded]] [[def-closed-manifold|closed]] surface $\Sigma$ in a $3$-manifold $M$ is *locally separating* if its normal bundle is trivial — equivalently, $\Sigma$ has a tubular neighborhood $\Sigma \times (-1, 1)$, so that a small neighborhood of $\Sigma$ minus $\Sigma$ has two components ($\Sigma$ is *two-sided*). Otherwise the normal bundle is the twisted $I$-bundle and $\Sigma$ is *one-sided*.

## Notes

> [!note]- Notes (click to expand)
> - Locally separating does not imply globally separating: a two-sided surface may still fail to disconnect $M$.
> - In an orientable $3$-manifold every two-sided surface is orientable; since $\mathbb{R}P^2$ is non-orientable, an orientable manifold contains no locally separating $\mathbb{R}P^2$ — which is why the hypothesis of [[thm-surgery-flow-exists]] is vacuous in the orientable case.

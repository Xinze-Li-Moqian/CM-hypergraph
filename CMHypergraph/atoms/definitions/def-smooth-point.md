# Definition — smooth point

The generic points of a space-time: those where nothing happens to the
topology, so that locally the space-time is just a slice moving in time.

## Statement

> [!definition] Smooth point
> Let ${\mathcal M}$ be a [[def-spacetime|space-time]], with time function $\mathbf{t}$ and vector field $\chi$. A point $x \in {\mathcal M}$ is a *smooth point* if it has a neighbourhood of product form $U \times J$ — with $U$ an open set of the time-slice through $x$ and $J$ an open interval — on which $\mathbf{t}$ is the projection to $J$ and the curves $\{y\} \times J$ are the integral curves of $\chi$.

## Notes

> [!note]- Notes (click to expand)
> - At a regular time every point of the slice is smooth: the product neighbourhoods glue into the product structure $M_t \times J$ over an interval of regular times.
> - The flow line of a smooth point extends both forward and backward in time, at least a little — this is what fails for the other two kinds of point of a surgery space-time.
